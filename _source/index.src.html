Preliminary results. Manuscript in preparation. Advisor: David Wilder, Rutgers University.

Time flies when you’re having zen.Eight minutes of meditation, remembered as five or six.

For my 2019 honors thesis, we had 214 Rutgers undergraduates listen to an eight-minute recording: either a guided meditation or a passage of The Hobbit audiobook. Afterward, with no warning that the question was coming, they estimated how long it had lasted.

I wanted to take a closer look at the methods and develop a deeper understanding of the statistical analysis, so I reanalyzed the original data from the raw files in R, adding effect sizes, Bayes factors and a full set of robustness checks. The main result held up, although some of my original explanations for it did not.

The design

Each participant was randomly assigned to one of four groups. The recording varied, as did attentional focus: on the self and body, or on a narrative. The body-and-breath meditation and The Hobbit audiobook come from Kramer et al. (2013); for my study, we also added a waterfall visualization and manipulated self-focused attention via a mirror.

54% of meditators chose 5–6 minutes. 70% of audiobook listeners chose the correct 7–8 minutes or longer, compared with 37% of meditators. Cohen's d = −0.63, 95% CI [−0.91, −0.36].

What the reanalysis found

Each result is reported with a “Bayes factor.” Unlike a p-value, this statistic helps us infer which explanation is actually favored, including “no effect” (as opposed to just “rejecting the null”).

How strong is the evidence for each result?

Bayes factors on a log scale. Left of 1 favors no effect, right of 1 favors an effect. Between 1/3 and 3 the data can't decide.

Meditation made eight minutes feel shorter

Meditators' estimates clustered around 5–6 minutes, while audiobook listeners' clustered around the correct 7–8. The difference was medium to large (d = −0.63; F(1, 210) = 23.13, p < .001). The Bayes factor favors a real effect by about 5,200 to 1. The effect appeared under both kinds of focus. It looked larger in the narrative-focus groups, but that difference wasn't reliable, so I don't interpret it.

Self-awareness did not explain it

My original hypothesis was that meditation raises self-awareness, and self-awareness stretches time. Body-focused meditation did raise state self-awareness a little, but self-awareness had no relationship with the duration estimates: r = .00, 95% CI [−.14, .13]. The Bayes factor favors no link by about 6 to 1. The mirror, meant to raise self-awareness without meditation (Phillips & Silvia, 2005), didn’t raise it.

A smaller echo in seconds

In each condition, participants were shown four shapes on screen for 7, 17, 37 and 57 seconds and estimated how long each lasted, before and after listening to their recording.

After meditating, their estimates fell further, to 80.7% of the true durations, compared with 87.4% after the audiobook, once each person's first estimates are taken into account (p = .016). The Bayes factor is only 2.4, so although the difference is statistically significant, the evidence for it is weak. The paper will treat this result as secondary.

Language after surgery and the CAM-ICU

Presented at Academic Evening, Columbia University Irving Medical Center, April 2020

How I work

Methods and tools

Research methods

Between-subjects experiments, retrospective and prospective timing tasks, and Qualtrics surveys. I analyze data in R and SPSS with ANOVA, mixed models, Bayes factors and nonparametric tests, and report effect sizes and confidence intervals. In the future, I hope to incorporate more ethnographic and qualitative methods, as well as text analysis with the Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count (LIWC) program.

Clinical and regulatory

I’ve set up EEG electrodes in the midst of open-heart surgery, consented surgical patients, and processed personnel modifications, continuing reviews and closures while ensuring studies comply with 45 CFR 46 (the Common Rule) and ICH-GCP.

Working with AI

Analysis scripts

Claude helped me write the reanalysis script, and I ran it myself in RStudio. Every result line states the value it should produce, so I could check each one as I went. Once we confirm that the original consent and IRB approval allow it, I’ll post the data and code so anyone can rerun the analysis from the raw files.

Citation checks

Claude helped me audit the citations and terminology in my 2019 thesis, which turned up eight errors to fix before I submit the paper to a journal.

About

I review human subjects research at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai and process approximately 150 submissions a month across behavioral and biomedical studies. Earlier, I spent two years in Columbia’s Department of Anesthesiology, coordinating studies of postoperative delirium and recovery of consciousness.

My research interests center on how morally and existentially significant experiences, from meditation to awe to psychedelic states, reshape cognition, identity and the sense of time. Recently, I earned a certificate in interdisciplinary psychedelic studies from Ohio State.

Outside work I shoot photos, play guitar, snowboard and scuba dive. If there’s a Phish show or a Grateful Dead cover band nearby, I’m probably there.
